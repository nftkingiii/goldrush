## 1. What the app lead already covers, and the gap

App: https://oathcastcourt.duckdns.org/gold-rush. Read-only GETs worked: `/gold-rush/api/sites` (8,132 gold workings: `site_no`, `name`, `sheet`, `lat`, `lon`, `band`), `/gold-rush/api/specialists` (miners whose RESEARCH_QUERY_GENERAL catalogue entry names gold, mining, geology or Queensland), `/gold-rush/api/ledger` (public paid calls).

- The app asks each specialist one fixed question: "Tell me about the Mount Morgan gold mine, Queensland: who owned it, how much gold it produced, and when and why it closed." (the earlier variant said "the MOUNT MORGAN gold mine near MOUNT MORGAN, Queensland"). It sends the name, not the site number.
- Each call is $0.01 test USDC from the app's escrow. Answers are shown as `answer`, `sources`, `brief`, plus a keyword check of four topics: ownership, production, closure, gold price. "nobody" marks a gap.
- Miner 64176 (OathCast Queensland Gold Mines) returns, for Mount Morgan: site number 494270, "abandoned mine", location, "giant, over 150 tonnes", other commodities, 37 other workings within 5 km, and "The record provides a size band but no production figures, owners, or closure dates." Its sources are the MINOCC ArcGIS query URL and the GSQ dataset page.
- Ledger observation: the first call to 64176 on 2026-11-20 13:33 UTC is recorded as `paid_refusal`, then a second call "served". Why is unknown (open question 4).

So the gap is real and matches our promise: production, closure reasons, ownership and gold price at the time are held by nobody.

**Conclusion.** No keyless open bulk dataset gives per-site reported production, closure reasons or ownership history for Queensland gold. The only open structured source (GA) gives current companies and resources. Production and closure text lives in (a) the full MINOCC/QMIN database, (b) QDEX/CR PDFs, (c) historic compilations such as MINDEP, and (d) narrative pages such as Heritage Register entries. (a) and (b) are behind a portal that refuses automated clients, so they need a person to download or an explicit decision on how to access them. The miner is therefore built so that coverage is a curated, quote-verified set that can grow without code changes; everything else answers `not_found`. (Update: the full MINOCC 2017 database was then supplied by hand; see section 5.)

## 3. Intents

Source: `GET https://devnode.telegraphprotocol.com/engine/v1/intents/<id>` and `/engine/v1/intents` (136 intents), `anjoman.ai/api/explorer/canonical-intents` (same names, 136).

- **RESEARCH_QUERY_GENERAL**: class `judgment`, 30 miners, lifecycle active. Request: `question` (string, required, on-chain `max_bytes` 256). Response fields: `answer` (string), `source_count` (integer 0-1000), `sources` (list of URLs or names), `brief` (string, at most 80 characters, processed on-chain). No comparator, no scored numeric field. Extra fields in our JSON are allowed off-chain.
- **CONTENT_EXTRACTION_GENERAL**: class `judgment`, 22 miners. Request: `content` (the content or a URL) and `fields` (what to extract). Response: `title`, `content`, `url`, `entities`, `brief`. It extracts from content the caller supplies, so it is not the home for a question about a mine. Possible later use: a caller supplies a CR report and asks for production and closure fields.
- **Historical gold price, best fit**: there is no commodity intent. Candidates:
  - FINANCIAL_DATA_GENERAL: concept fits ("market data ... beyond a single quoted price") but it is `deterministic` with a comparator on `market_cap_usd` (code 2, 200 bps, keys Bitcoin and Ethereum), request has only `asset`, no date. A gold-price miner would be tested on Bitcoin market caps and fail.
  - MACRO_ECONOMIC_INDICATOR_GENERAL: `deterministic`, `indicator` is an enum with the single value "unemployment rate", requires `country` and `year`.
  - CRYPTO_PRICE_USD, STOCK_PRICE_US_EQUITY, FX_NOW_*: current price only, other assets.
  - **Chosen: RESEARCH_QUERY_GENERAL.** Judgment class, free-text question carries the month or range, `sources` carries the series URL, and it is the intent the app already calls.
- Miner YAML and registration docs read: yaml-config, miner-registration, validation-api (the public validate endpoint answers 404, so validation is the web interface at anjoman.ai/dashboard/earn or a `registerMiner` dry run). Rules that shaped the YAMLs: intents only (no prefixes); each endpoint needs `description`, `intents`, `params`; schema blocks are closed sets; `input_schema` is top-level only; `intent_mapping` is optional and lets the node read `answer`, `source_count`, `sources` by path.

## 4. What was built

Folder `C:\Users\USER\Documents\Telegraph\GoldRush`, own local git repo, no remote.

- `src/app.ts`, `answer.ts`, `match.ts`, `price.ts`, `store.ts`: question to site resolution, record assembly, price lookup. `src/sources.ts`, `zip.ts`: parsers for GSQ, GA, Pink Sheet (XLSX read with an in-repo ZIP reader) and RBA, with injected `fetch`. `src/server.ts`: `GET /ask`, `GET /price`, `/health`, the two YAMLs, per-IP limit of 30 a minute. The service reads only its local snapshot; it makes no outbound call while answering.
- `data/index.json` (8,132 GSQ gold sites, 288 GA deposits, dataset names, licences and fetch dates), `data/gold-monthly.json`, `data/evidence.json` (25 curated records, 4 sources), `data/raw/` (RBA values, GA rows). `scripts/` rebuilds each of them.
- `miners/goldrush-qld-history.yaml` (id 7310050) and `miners/goldrush-gold-price.yaml` (id 7310051). Both free on the devnode list on 2026-11-20.
- `eval/pairs.jsonl`: 18 preference pairs, `scripts/make-eval-pairs.ts` regenerates them.

Rules the code enforces: every production, closure and ownership row carries `source_ref`, `source_url` and a verbatim `quote`, and a test checks each quote is in the recorded page; closure `reason_class` is `unknown` unless the quoted text states the cause; records from different sources are never merged (conflicts are listed, all values kept); no evidence means `not_found`; a name that exists only on a different 1:100,000 map sheet than the one asked about is refused; same-name same-size sites are refused as ambiguous.

Honest limits: curated coverage is Mount Morgan (494270), Day Dawn lode (38730, three mines on the lode) and Brilliant lode (38722). The 8,129 other sites answer with the GSQ record and `not_found`. Australian dollar prices are derived (World Bank monthly average divided by RBA month-end rate) and are labelled so; the pre-1960 gold price is `not_found`.

## 5. MINOCC 2017 database integration (added 2026-11-20)

**Input.** `queensland-mineral-occurrence-data.zip` (44.6 MB, dataset ds000004 "Queensland Mineral Occurrence Data", downloaded by the user from the GSQ portal). Treated as untrusted data: extracted into its own empty folder (`data/raw/qmin-zip/` locally, `~/goldrush/raw/qmin/` on the testbench VM), read only with `mdb-tables`, `mdb-schema` and `mdb-export` (mdbtools 1.0.0, from the Ubuntu package), nothing executed. Contents: `MINOCC 2017.mdb` (267,104,256 bytes, sha256 `86a530cbaa8a0c9f04a66900f36643ec4980f6f7a1d25b199e3e7fdd08abbe80`, Access 2000) and three PDFs in `User_information/`. On the VM the extracted `.mdb`, the CSV exports and the zip were deleted afterwards (mdbtools stays installed); the zip is kept only in the user's Downloads. The `.mdb`, the zip's extraction and the CSV exports are git-ignored; only the derived JSON, the rebuild script and small test fixtures are committed.

**The PDFs.** `MINOCC_digital_data_Version_April_2017_reduced.pdf` is a one-page coverage map of Queensland by 1:100,000 sheet ("QUEENSLAND MINERAL OCCURRENCE DIGITAL DATA, Current March 2017"), not a data dictionary: it has no field definitions. `MINOCC reports list.pdf` lists the source reports by sheet area; its first paragraph reads: "Digital data for all mines documented in the MINOCC reports listed below are available on this DVD product from the Geological Survey of Queensland (GSQ). The data are in an Access 2000 database with ESRI shapefile or MapInfo format files generated from this database." `History of Minocc mapping.pdf` is the history of the programme (data compiled from published and unpublished literature and field investigations; location accuracy from about 100 m to under 10 m). **None of the three contains a licence, copyright or attribution statement** (searched for licen*, copyright, creative, attribution, disclaimer). The only licence evidence remains the portal listing read earlier: dataset ds000004, "License Creative Commons Attribution 4.0". Attribution carried by every MINOCC record: dataset title and version (MINOCC 2017, digital data version April 2017), "(c) State of Queensland (Department of Natural Resources and Mines)", CC BY 4.0, and the dataset page https://geoscience.data.qld.gov.au/dataset/ds000004. That page URL is the dataset id from the listing; it was not fetched (the portal refuses automated clients). Field meanings below come from `mdb-schema` and the data, since no data dictionary text was supplied.

Not used: `Biblio` (62,844 references; CR numbers are the report keys asked for), `Text` (free-text summaries such as "produced ~8Moz of gold": unstructured, left out rather than parsed), `Resources`, the geology tables.

**Join.** `SITE_NO` is the GSQ `site_no` of the ArcGIS layer our index uses. Verified: Mount Morgan 494270 = "MOUNT MORGAN", Day Dawn 38730 = "DAY DAWN LODE", Brilliant 38722 = "BRILLIANT LODE" in `Location`, the same names as the index. Of the 8,132 indexed gold sites, 8,130 are in `Location` (missing: 517058 and 610384); names are equal for 8,110 and differ for 20 (the database says "UNNAMED nnnnnn" where the layer has a name).

**Record identity.** The tables have no row id, so `source_ref` is `MINOCC-2017:<table>:<SITE_NO>[:<row>]`, where `<row>` is the 1-based position among the site's rows in the exported table (`Production`, `Explorer`) or MINOCC's own `Sequence Number` (`MineHist`). Each record also has `source_table`, `record_id`, a `quote` of the recorded text and `source_url` = the GSQ dataset page.

**Rules kept.** MINOCC records are separate source records, never merged with the curated, Queensland Government, Wikipedia or GA ones. Units are as recorded: Mount Morgan gold is `294056 kg`, not tonnes or ounces; an ounce reading appears only in a separate `converted` field labelled "explicit conversion for reading only: 1 troy oz = 31.1034768 g". Conflicts are found across sources at whole-site level, comparing in tonnes (conversion for the comparison only) with a 1% tolerance, and every value is listed. Mount Morgan gold: 250 t (Queensland Government, 1882-1990), 262 t (Wikipedia, 1882-1981), 294.056 t (MINOCC, recorded as 294056 kg, period not stated; its four dated Production rows add up to the same 294056 kg). Mount Morgan closure: 1990, 1981 and 1990 (MINOCC MineHist row 6, 1981-1990). MINOCC closure `reason_class` is always `unknown`, with no `reason_text`: the latest End Year in `MineHist` says when the last recorded period of working ended, not why. A MineHist comment that does name a cause (Mount Morgan: "Mine workings devastated by fire in 1925 and in conjunction with worker unrest mine closed in 1927") is carried verbatim in `years_worked` and not classified. In `Production`, the date "1900-01-00" means unknown (null); financial-year rows (1 July to 30 June) give the two calendar years. A total gets a period only when every Production row of the site is dated at that end; otherwise null. A lode total (MINOCC Day Dawn lode, 38,668 kg) is not compared with the per-mine ounces of the Heritage Register text: different scopes, both kept.

**Rebuild.** `node scripts/build-minocc.ts --mdb "<path>/MINOCC 2017.mdb"` (needs `mdb-export`; run on a Linux box with mdbtools, 31 s) or `--csv-dir <dir>` for CSVs exported with `mdb-export -T "%Y-%m-%d" -D "%Y-%m-%d"`. **Response schema** (the app lead's request): `site_no` (not `site_id`), `site_name`, `production`, `closure`, `years_worked`, `ownership`, `references`, `conflicts`, `not_found` and, for price questions, `gold_price` (`month` = "YYYY-MM") are top-level keys next to `answer`, `sources`, `source_count` and `brief`. Production and ownership periods are integer years; `basis` stays "reported" or "estimated"; `not_found` is unchanged. The key facts are also in `answer`, in words with the source named.

### 6.2 Design

Live point queries at answer time (`src/land.ts`): per layer, one point-in-polygon query and one query with `distance=2000` metres; a record found by both is `contains_site_point`, otherwise `within_buffer`. Timeout 8 s per query, six queries in flight at a time, per-site per-layer cache with a 24 h TTL (`checked_at` is the fetch time; a hit keeps its original `checked_at`). A layer that fails or times out is listed under `not_found` with the reason, contributes no rows, and is not cached; a stale value is never served as current. At most 25 rows per layer are listed (over-the-point first) and the cut is stated in `notes`. The earlier "no outbound call while answering" property no longer holds for land and rights questions; price, site and history answers still read only the local snapshots.
`gold_price_real` uses a dated snapshot, `data/cpi.json` (`scripts/fetch-cpi.ts`, fetched 2026-11-21), and its rows carry that snapshot date as `checked_at`. Closure year = the latest End Year of the site's MINOCC `MineHist` rows (the response says so and lists other sources' years without using them); closure-year price = mean of that year's monthly prices (World Bank average from 1960, official US price from 1900-03 before that); real value = price times latest CPI over the year's average CPI; "today" = the latest month held by both gold and CPI (2026-10). Before 1913 there is no CPI-U so the answer is `not_found`; a site with no MINOCC closure year is `not_found`. A$ is the existing derived figure (nominal only, RBA from 1970) and is null earlier. The real figure is labelled "US-dollar purchasing power".
